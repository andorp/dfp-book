# Chapter 16 — *Inside the Clifford World*: companion code

There are five challenge files, one for each dialogue challenge. Each file checks its claim
**twice**. The blackboard does it with NumPy `assert`s. The sketchbook compares PyZX diagrams
as tensors through `tools/dfp.py`'s `matrix_of` / `same_picture`, which force
`strategy="naive"`. Where the chapter derives an equality by rules, the file derives it too,
with the named rewrites of `pyzx.simplify`: `color_change`, `fuse`, `copy`, `spider_simp`,
`id_simp`, `lcomp` / `lcomp_simp`, `pivot_simp` and `clifford_simp`. Running a file is the
book's analogue of type-checking.

```bash
# one challenge at a time
.venv/bin/python chapter16/Code/Ch16/SafeTouches.py
# the whole chapter (must end "5 files, 0 failed")
bash check-code.sh 16
```

Each file also runs `pictures()`, at the end of its blackboard. The dialogue's prop pictures
(`\cradlemodel`, `\belltray`, `\bowcloth`, `\trayledger`, `\eighthcloth`: twenty calls, four
per challenge) are read out of `dialog.md` and `dialog.raw.md` through `tools/dfp_props.py`.
Their state is computed by `tools/props_ch16.py`, a transcription of `pdf/props/ch16.lua` (with
the shared cloth and Office kits). The transcription carries a drift guard against the Lua's
numbers and code, and where the Lua has values still being tuned (the clip pair's offsets, the
"ring" word's drop, the tassel's and the ledger names' places) it reads them from the Lua instead
of pinning them. It was diffed against the Lua under texlua: the 20 reference calls, 140 error
calls and 4080 fuzzed calls give 0 differences (in three fuzzed calls the kit itself reports no
room for a keyed word, an allowed fault), and every one of the 45 `tex.error` sites is reached.
`pictures()` ties each picture to the blackboard. A model's lug holds $U^\dagger PU$, a clip's
turn is a phase, a tray's touch is a string of Paulis, the ledger's rows are the stabilizers,
and a cloth is a net with its number. It also checks the dialogue's rules: blocks and sizes,
captions in words only and no echoes, the anchor phrases, the starred pictures (P1.1, P2.1,
P2.2, P4.1, P5.2), and every keyed word's clearance on drawn extents. A word's node box clears
the frame by at least $0.025$ cm, and its letters (glyph ink boxes measured from the book's
font) clear every part drawn, every container's edge and every other word by at least $0.02$
cm. Five words come nearer to one neighbour each and are held clear of it by a positive gap:
the sleeve's word ($0.0197$ above its sleeve's shadow) and the four wing bells' names in the
ledger ($0.0127$ between the rows' names, a descender over an ascender). This is a structural
check of the pictures, not a third proof.

Five things hold in every file.

- **Scalars are exact.** As in Chapters 9–15, the "up to a non-zero scalar" licence is never
  used. A Hadamard **edge** (the yellow box on a tie) is exactly $H$. The bare net of $v$
  bells and $e$ ties is exactly $2^{(v-e)/2}\ket G$, and every picture that is compared
  with a net carries that number, as `add_power(e' - e)` (a power of $\sqrt2$). A spider
  with no legs and phase $\alpha$ is the number $1+e^{i\alpha}$.
- **Top wire first.** The bow tie's bells in ket order are left-back, left-front, the knot,
  right-back, right-front (`0 … 4` in code), with ties `(0,1),(0,2),(1,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,4)`.
  $K_3=Z\otimes Z\otimes X\otimes Z\otimes Z$ is asserted, and so is the fact that $K_1$
  read bottom-up is a different string.
- **Generic inputs where the claim is general.** Every "for every graph" of the chapter is
  checked on the bow tie and on seeded random graphs of up to six vertices; the injection
  on random **non-real** data states. $S$ and $T$ are diagonal, hence symmetric, and are
  never used as a generic matrix.
- **Negative controls.** The colour-swapped touch, the half-touch with both clips turned the
  same way, the pivot without its $Z$s, the LC-rule without its phase, the ledger's rules
  without their sign flips, the lit injection branch without its mending quarter: each is
  asserted to fail.
- **No picture proves a bound.** The shrink is checked as a chain of equalities that ends
  in a number; nothing in the files claims a running time.

## The Twenty-Four — `TwentyFour.py`

Achilles counts six tips up and five in front, thirty, and finds that the tip opposite the
top is always in the socket: six times four. Then he walks the Crab's model through every
seating once and home. The file enumerates the group generated by $H$ and $S$ by its
**rotations** $R_{ij}=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}(P_iUP_jU^\dagger)$, which are blind to a phase,
and finds exactly the twenty-four signed permutations of determinant $+1$. It names the
seating of each element by its up tip and front tip, with the book's colours ($+Z$ dark
green, $+X$ dark red, $+Y$ dark gold, minus signs pale), and replays the Crab's calls
(quarter $=S$, back $=S^\dagger$, over $=H$, each multiplying on the left). The result is
Achilles's list, line for line:

```python
assert seen == WALK_NAMES                    # the card's 24, in order
assert len(set(seen)) == 24                  # every key once
assert key(rotation(U)) == key(rotation(I2)) # and home again
assert walks((Rs, Rh)) == (0, 6)             # quarter and over alone: six open paths, no way home
```

The blackboard also counts the Pauli group ($4\cdot4^n$ for $n=1,2,3$) and checks the
anticommutation criterion of Result 16.1 on every pair of strings on two and three wires.
It shows that the half-turns are the Paulis (diagonal rotations), and that one quarter
from home gives `Dark green up, pale gold front.`, not the back move's `dark gold`. The
sketchbook builds $S$, $X$, $Z$ and $Y=i\,$(green $\pi$ then red $\pi$) as spiders, exactly,
and the CNOT as $\sqrt2$ times the green–red pair. It draws all twenty-four as one-wire
$H$/$S$ circuits, checking that every phase is a multiple of a quarter, and **derives** the
"over" as colour change: `simp.color_change` on a red $\pi$ gives the green $\pi$ between
two yellow boxes.

**The pictures.** P1.1–P1.4 are the cradle model. Each lug's tip is $U^\dagger PU$, with front
$+X$, right $+Y$, back $-X$, left $-Y$ and up $+Z$, a dark tip $+$ and a pale tip $-$. This is
asserted for every place of the four dumps, and the conjugation the other way round
($UPU^\dagger$) names a different tip. The
walk is replayed through the transcription's `model_state`: twenty-five steps, twenty-four
different seatings, back home, named exactly as Achilles names them. The Opening's D1–D7
phrases each occur once, before the First Challenge, in both files. P1.1's keyed words
clear everything, the stand and lugs stay fixed, and the frame is $2.07$ cm.

## Safe Touches — `SafeTouches.py` (the headline and the master)

The Crab clips the knot's touch onto his tray, and the Tortoise pushes the red half into
the knot on her cloth and watches it vanish in pairs. The blackboard proves
$K_a\ket G=\ket G$ and $U_GX_aU_G^\dagger=K_a$ for every bell of the bow tie and every
vertex of thirty random graphs. It checks all of Result 16.7 (the $K_a$ commute; $2^n$
distinct products, none $-I$; the basis $Z^{\mathbf b}\ket G$; the projector) and its
uniqueness clause, as the joint $+1$ space having rank one. The two-touch case is
$K_1K_4=X_1Z_2X_4Z_5$. An $X_a$ alone, and the touch with its colours swapped, are noticed.

The sketchbook builds *The Touched Bow Tie* with a clip ring on every output, unfused:

```python
assert g.num_edges() == 16                   # 6 ties + 5 inner + 5 outer lengths
assert same_picture(master, untouched)
assert np.allclose(matrix_of(master).ravel(), 2 ** -0.5 * G)
assert not same_picture(net(5, BOWTIE, swapped)[0], untouched)   # colours swapped: no
```

Result 16.5 (row 120) is checked for $k=2,\dots,6$ legs in both colours. A turned spider
fails it. The rule is also **derived** as in the chapter's sidebar: a red point is fed
in, and `spider_simp` then `copy` on one side, `copy` then `spider_simp` on the other,
end in the same $k-1$ red points with the same number. The net's number $2^{(v-e)/2}$ is
checked on forty random graphs. Finally the §16.7 proof is carried out as **edits of the
master**, with `same_picture` after each: the knot's red $\pi$ is moved onto its four ties
(row 120), each crosses its box by `simp.color_change` (row 58), and each is `simp.fuse`d
into its wing bell together with that bell's green $\pi$ (row 57). The end is all phases
zero, six yellow boxes, scalar $1$: the untouched net.

**The pictures.** P2.1 is the tray at rest. The knot's touch, red at the knot and green on
its four ties, is $K_3=Z\otimes Z\otimes X\otimes Z\otimes Z$, which fixes $\ket G$, and every
bell's touch is safe. The sixteen cords (five inner, five outer, six ties) are the same in
every tray picture. P2.2 is the bow-tie net on the cloth, exactly $2^{-1/2}\ket G$. P2.3 is
$K_3$ applied to it. P2.4 is the net again, with no counter aside, and `same_picture` holds
against the net its dump describes. A wrongly threaded net fails. The kit sets P2.2's own
words (`tie`, `tube`, `ring`, `loose cord`). Its placement is not transcribed, so their
clearances were measured from the Lua's output, after the kit's raise of the words above the
linen: all at least $0.024$ cm (`loose cord` now clears the linen's edge by $0.052$). The rings stay at their places from picture to picture.

## Half a Touch — `HalfATouch.py`

Quarter clips on the left-back bell and its neighbours re-tie the net without moving a
cord, and a second set of quarters makes the whole touch. The blackboard checks the worked
example ($UK_2U^\dagger=K'_1K'_2$ of the bow tie without the tie $2$–$3$). It checks
Result 16.8 with its exact phase and its square on every bell and on a hundred random
cases, and the proof's operator identities ($K_c\mapsto K'_c$ off the neighbourhood,
$K_b\mapsto K'_aK'_b$ on it):

```python
assert np.allclose(Ua @ Gs, phase * graph_state(m, Et))       # e^{i pi (|N_a|-1)/4}
assert not np.allclose(Ua @ Gs, graph_state(m, Et))           # when that phase is not 1
assert np.allclose(Ua @ Ua, 1j ** (d - 1) * K(m, E, a))
```

It checks the clip rings' matrices, both directions of Result 16.9 (the same direction
twice fails), and the pivot of Result 16.10 on every edge of eighty random graphs. For the
pivot it confirms the $A$/$B$/$C$ description of the new graph and the identity
$e^{-i\frac\pi4X}e^{i\frac\pi4Z}e^{-i\frac\pi4X}=-YH$; without $Z_C$ the pivot fails. For
Result 16.11 it enumerates the stabilizer states: $6$, $60$, $1080$, which is Aaronson and
Gottesman's formula, and $2\,423\,520$ for five. Each of the six and sixty is fixed by
exactly $2^n$ signed Paulis. For $n=2,3$ the enumerated states are exactly the graph
states with one of the twenty-four on each wire (the GSLC form).

The sketchbook checks the net forms with their numbers: $2^{-1/2}$ at the left-back bell
and $2$ at the knot. It fuses two quarter clips into the safe touch (`simp.fuse`), checks
the pivot's net rule, and **derives** the interior form: a green quarter-round cap on the
left-back bell, `spider_simp`, then `lcomp_simp` leaves the re-tied net of the other four
bells, the neighbours a quarter back, times $e^{i\pi/4}/2$. The boundary form is checked by
matrix, not derived, as the chapter says.

**The pictures.** P3.1 is half the left-back's touch. A red clip a quarter round is
$e^{i\pi/4}e^{-i\pi X/4}$, and a green clip a quarter back is $\mathrm{diag}(1,-i)$, not
$\mathrm{diag}(1,i)$. These sit on the left-back and on its two tied bells. P3.3 adds a second
clip of each: two make $X$ and $Z$ exactly, and the tray's touch is $K_1$. P3.2 is the cloth
re-tied at the left-back. The ties are the vee, local complementation at the left-back (the
left-front–knot tie lost and laid at the near edge), with one counter aside. The half touch
takes the bare bow-tie net to exactly $2^{-1/2}$ times the bare vee net, and without the
counter it fails. P3.4 is back at the bow tie, with the counter taken back. The half touch
takes the vee net to $2^{1/2}$ times the bow-tie net, so the two numbers multiply to $1$. The
counter lies below the linen and its shadow, inside the frame and clear of every ring and
cord. The laid tie and its tube lie on the linen. Where two clips share a ring, the first
keeps exactly its single-clip place (P3.3 against P3.1), and the second stands towards the
side rim: at its left on the left wing and the knot, as D15 says, and at its right on the
right wing. The two clear each other, strokes and shadows included, by at least $0.015$ cm (measured: $0.018$ at the
knot, $0.020$ and $0.031$ elsewhere). No clip touches its bell, pad, pad shadow or crown,
and every clip lies inside the tray's outline.

## The Crab's Ledger — `CrabsLedger.py`

The Crab writes five rows and shows one update; the Tortoise shrinks her cloth net to lone
rings. The file's `Ledger` class keeps $n$ rows of $x$-bits, $z$-bits and a sign, with
Aaronson and Gottesman's rules for $H$, $S$, CNOT and CZ. It asserts the tray's five rows
in the Crab's colours and the §16.4 table after $H$ on the knot. Then it runs the ledger
beside the state vector through five random forty-gate quarter-turn circuits:

```python
psi = apply(led, psi, random_gate(rng, n))
assert led.fixes(psi)                         # every row, after every gate
```

Dropping the sign flip from the $H$ rule or the $S$ rule is caught. Result 16.13 (iv) is
checked on random Pauli measurements of the final states: each has odds exactly $\tfrac12$
when it anticommutes with a row, and is certain otherwise, $+1$ exactly when $M$ is in the
group. The counts are asserted: $n(2n+1)=55$, $100\cdot201=20\,100$,
`len(str(2 ** 100)) == 31`, and $2n(2n+1)=110$.

The sketchbook checks Result 16.14. The quarter-capped bow tie is exactly $1+i$. The three
hand-built pictures (the caps fused, then each pull-out, each worth $e^{i\pi/4}/2$) are
compared each with the one before. `spider_simp` + `lcomp_simp` empties the picture to the
scalar $1+i$; pulling out the knot first (`simp.lcomp`) leaves a different picture, a ring
of four at rest, which `pivot_simp` shrinks to the same number. With caps at rest the value
is $1$ and `pivot_simp` empties it. Twelve seeded random closed Clifford circuits
(`cliffordT(4, 40, p_t=0)`, closed by $\ket{0000}$ and $\bra{0000}$) all shrink under
`clifford_simp` to no vertices at all, and the scalar left is $\bra{0000}C\ket{0000}$.

**The pictures.** P4.1 is the ledger's page. Its rows `rgg--+;grg--+;ggrgg+;--grg+;--ggr+`
(red $X$, green $Z$, gold $Y$, blank $I$, the sign plain) are $K_1,\dots,K_5$, each fixing the
tray. The same colours read bottom-up, or read red-for-$Z$, fail. P4.2 copies the rows with an
over on the knot. Each new row is $HK_aH^\dagger$, so the knot's column swaps red and green and
the signs stay plain. P4.1's keyed words (the five bells' names and `sign`) clear the page and
each other at letter level. P4.3 caps every loose cord a quarter round. The closed net is
exactly $1+i$, and the same net capped a quarter back is not. P4.4 pulls out the left-back
and the right-back. That leaves three lone rings, worth $2$, $1-i$ and $2$, and two counters
of $e^{i\pi/4}/2$ each, so $2(1-i)2\cdot(e^{i\pi/4}/2)^2=1+i$. Without both counters this fails.

## An Eighth — `AnEighth.py`

The Tortoise calls an eighth, the model will not sit, and the Crab's box of eighth-ends
brings one in from outside. The blackboard checks $TXT^\dagger=(X+Y)/\sqrt2$ (two Pauli
components, so no phase times a Pauli), that $T$'s rotation is none of the twenty-four,
and $T^2=S$. It checks the model's tips after one eighth: the front lug meets the edge
between the dark red and the pale gold,

```python
assert np.allclose(RT @ [1, 0, 0], np.array([1, 1, 0]) / s2)
assert np.allclose(RT @ [0, -1, 0], np.array([1, -1, 0]) / s2)
assert np.allclose(mid / np.linalg.norm(mid), [1, 0, 0])
```

and it checks Bravyi and Kitaev's counts on the model: an edge midpoint has $12$ images,
a face centre $8$. $\ket\mu$ points at the edge midpoint between $+X$ and $+Y$ (H-type).
Their $T$ is a Clifford that cycles the axes. Their $\Lambda(e^{-i\pi/4})$ is our
$T^\dagger$. The injection is run on random non-real states: $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}T\ket\psi$ on
reading $0$, $\tfrac{e^{i\pi/4}}{\sqrt2}T\ket\psi$ on reading $1$ after $S$, odds
$\tfrac12$ each. Without $S$, or with $S^\dagger$, the lit branch is not $T\ket\psi$.

The sketchbook shows the eighth stopping the shrink. The bow tie capped with eighths has
the chapter's value, and `clifford_simp` leaves all five $\pi/4$ spiders inside, value
kept. Twenty seeded Clifford$+T$ circuits keep their value under `clifford_simp`, and every
one that it does not reduce to a number is stuck at an eighth. (A few circuits do reach a
number: there the pull-outs remove every neighbour of each eighth, and an eighth left with
no legs is simply a number, $1+e^{i\pi/4}$. No eighth is ever pulled out.) Each injection branch picture equals $e^{ia\pi/4}T$, which is $\sqrt2$
times the blackboard's branch. Reading $0$ is **derived** by `spider_simp` and `id_simp`
down to a single green $\pi/4$. Reading $1$ is derived by fusion, the $\pi$-commutation
into the magic point with its number $e^{i\pi/4}$ (a hand edit checked by `same_picture`;
without the number it fails), and two more fusions.

**The pictures.** P5.1 is the model an eighth from home. The lug at each side place holds
$T^\dagger PT=(P_1+P_2)/\sqrt2$, the two tips the dump names (front: between dark red and pale
gold, $(X-Y)/\sqrt2$). No side lug holds a Pauli, while up and the socket keep theirs. Two
eighths seat it as one quarter. P5.2 is the joiner with the eighth-end, whose bead at an
eighth is the green $\pi/4$ point $\ket0+e^{i\pi/4}\ket1$. Its net, with the far end closed by
a red end-ring at rest ($\sqrt2\bra0$) or at a half ($\sqrt2\bra1$), is the injection picture
of the sketchbook. P5.3, the dark reading, is exactly $T\ket\psi$ on a random non-real
$\ket\psi$, with no counter. P5.4, the lit reading, has one counter, $e^{i\pi/4}$: the counter
times the net is $S$ after the lit branch, $e^{i\pi/4}T\ket\psi$, and without the quarter or
the counter it fails. Every step of either reading keeps the value, counted with its
counters. The kit sets `end-ring`, `tie` and `cord` (measured from the Lua's output, at least
$0.019$ cm: `cord` to its cord). The Lua sets `ring` itself, below the red joiner. Its letters
clear the joiner by $0.038$, the cord by $0.097$ and the linen's edge by $0.157$ (asserted at
least $0.02$). This file also checks the
whole dialogue against both files: the five sections in order, twenty pictures all inside
them, the starred pictures, the card's negative anchors (comments stripped), the §1c form
check (twenty-four seatings, one `An eighth.`), the signal grep, no "Let Bob draw" and no
Genie.

The files begin on one wire: twenty-four seatings, where every quarter-turn picture
lives. They go on to the bow tie of bells, whose safe touches are its stabilizers, and to
the half-touch that re-ties a net without moving a cord. The ledger and the shrink then
follow any picture of quarters and halves on paper, down to a number. The last file shows
the eighth that no seating, ledger or shrink can hold, and how a ready-made eighth is
brought in. There is no proof-assistant tier; `bash check-code.sh 16` is the chapter's
check.
